### Rose:
The highlight from the previous FOSS session, such as something you found particularly interesting. This could also be something you're excited to implement now.

I didn't realize until this FOSS session that, if I'm understanding correctly, someone can use codespaces as an alternative to running docker on their computer using the terminal. This stood out to me because, especially in my field, I think there will be many more people who may be interested in running or exploring someone else's data, but they won't have a computer that is able to host/run/create the requisite environment on their computers locally. All to say, very cool to learn yet another way that we can interact with data without a cost/resource barrier. 

### Bud: 
Something that you are looking forward to digging into deeper. This could also be ideas on how to apply concepts to your current or future research projects.

Candidly, I'm not confident that creating containers will be something that researchers in the legal field will be doing in the near future given how new open science concepts are to the legal field. That said, soemthing I'm interested in understanding better are how environments and containers differ and how they interact. For example, a question that I have, is when using the ENV command in codespaces, where is the environment that its calling actually located? I'm trying to better understand this balance/interaction so that I don't fall into a trap of storing a fie or specification locally when it might need to be available to someone else running my code.

When using the "from command" to build a container, what considerations should be given to the container your building from? Also, where can you find example containers to be able to identify which one might be the most efficient for you to use?
